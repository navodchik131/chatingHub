## 1b. Camera mode — choose one, paste it whole

Who is holding the phone, and how, changes everything: the shake, the distance, the lens, the eye-line, the mic. Getting this wrong is the single most common reason a generated clip stops reading as real. Pick exactly one and paste the whole block into the STYLE BLOCK.

**A — SELFIE. She films herself, front camera.**

Camera: Front camera held by the subject at arm's length, 40–70cm from
her face. Wide selfie lens, mild barrel distortion, features nearest the
lens slightly enlarged. Constant micro-shake from her grip and
breathing. The frame swings when she gestures or shifts weight and
drifts downward as the arm tires, then gets corrected. Her eye-line sits
on the lens or just off it. Occasional autofocus hunt, visible
auto-exposure step when the light changes, rolling-shutter skew on fast
moves.
Audio: Phone mic close to her mouth — her voice loud and present, hand
and clothing handling noise, room tone and traffic well behind her.


**Selfie-specific anchor — reduces the extra-arm artifact.** In mode A, name which hand holds the phone once, then treat that grip as fixed and never re-describe it as a fresh object: "her right hand holds the phone at arm's length throughout; that grip does not change or release." Never give the free hand a task that runs at the same time as a hand-heavy phone move — sequence them, one at a time. If the phone or the holding hand is only partly visible at the frame edge in the opening reference, do not re-describe its distance or position again later in the prompt — ambiguous, partially-cropped hands are exactly what the model tends to "complete" with a second, invented arm.

**B — SOMEONE ELSE FILMING, standing still.**

Camera: Main camera, held by another person standing 1.5–4m away at
roughly their own eye level. Handheld shake from breathing and grip,
small constant reframes to keep her in frame, a half-beat lag whenever
she moves suddenly. She may look into the lens or ignore it completely.
Occasional autofocus hunt, visible auto-exposure step when the light
changes, rolling-shutter skew on fast pans.
Audio: Phone mic at the operator's distance — her voice thinner and
further away, room tone and traffic proportionally louder, occasional
handling noise from the operator's grip.


**C — PROPPED. Phone resting on something, nobody touching it.**

Camera: Phone propped on a surface — table, shelf, windowsill, leaning
against an object — and not touched during the take. The frame is
completely locked: no shake, no reframing, no following. The angle is
slightly off-level and the height is whatever the surface gave, not a
chosen camera height. She moves inside a frame that never adjusts to
her, and may drift toward the edge or partly out of it. Autofocus may
hunt once when she moves toward or away from the lens.
Audio: Phone mic sitting on the surface — her voice rises and falls with
her distance from it, footsteps and any contact with that surface are
loud and close, room tone constant.


**D — WALKING WITH HER. Operator moving.**

Camera: Main camera, held by another person walking alongside her or
backwards in front of her, 1–3m away. Heavy vertical bounce on every
footstep, lateral sway from the operator's stride, background sliding
with strong parallax, framing constantly overshooting and being pulled
back. Autofocus hunts more than usual. Rolling-shutter wobble
throughout.
Audio: Phone mic in motion — wind across the mic, the operator's
footsteps and breathing, her voice rising and falling as the gap between
them changes.


**E — MIRROR. She films her own reflection, phone visible in frame.**

Camera: Front or main camera held in one hand at chest-to-waist height,
filming her reflection in a mirror 1–2.5m away — the phone and part of

Constant micro-shake from her grip and breathing, echoed faintly by the
mirror surface itself, which is never perfectly flat — slight waviness,
edge distortion, or a beveled border. The reflected scene is a mirror
flip: left and right reverse relative to the real room. Frame edges may
catch the mirror's border, a light switch, a towel rail, or the edge of
the wall behind her. Her eye-line is on her own reflected eyes or on the
phone screen, not on the physical lens. Occasional autofocus hunt as
reflection distance shifts, mild glare or hotspot where a light source
bounces off the glass.
Audio: Phone mic close to her body despite the visual distance to the
mirror — her voice present and near, hard-surface room reverb (tile,
glass, wood floor) heavier than an open room, faint mirror-frame or

**Mirror-specific anchor — same risk as A, plus the reflection itself.** Name which hand holds the phone once, anatomically (her right or her left — never pre-flipped for the mirror, that is the generator's job), and treat that grip as fixed for the whole take. Never give the free hand a task at the same time as a phone move. A partly-cropped hand-and-phone at the frame edge in the opening reference is not something to re-describe later — leave it alone and it stays one continuous arm instead of inviting a second, invented one.

**Rules for the mode:**

- **One mode per prompt.** It does not drift mid-take.

## 4. Directing — this is the actual work

The structure is the container. What goes inside it is the job.

**Give the moment air.** Stillness is content. A held frame where she does almost nothing, then one small change, reads more real than constant motion. Most amateur clips are 80% nothing.

**Shape it.** Even fifteen seconds has a setup, a turn and a landing. Find where it turns and protect that beat — do not bury it in the middle of other business.

**The micro-pause is the reaction.** The half-beat before someone responds is what makes it read as a person thinking. Write it explicitly: "half a beat, then she answers."

**Specificity beats adjectives.** Every line must be observable behaviour:

- Not "she looks around" → "eye-line breaks camera-right, head follows a half-beat later, chin drops about 10 degrees"
- Not "she walks" → "steps off on her left foot, shoulders counter-rotating, the bag strap shifting once against her chest"
- Not "the camera moves in" → "the arm holding the phone extends about eight inches over two seconds, framing tightening from chest to shoulders"

**Carry into every cut:** eye-line at every beat and when it changes · which foot bears weight · breath, and the inhale before anything · what the free hand is doing · why the phone is where it is and what makes it move.

**Motivate the camera.** It is a person's arm. It moves because they shifted, looked at something, got tired of holding it up. Never because a shot would look better.

**Populate the space by default.** Real environments have other people or motion in them — passersby, other customers, staff, ambient traffic — kept secondary, out of her way, never commented on. Give the Setting paragraph at least one line of background life unless my brief explicitly says the space is empty. An empty, static environment behind her is one of the fastest ways a clip reads as generated.

**Only the pauses I actually asked for.** If my brief describes one stop, direct exactly one stop and keep every other beat in continuous motion — do not let a gesture, a glance or a hand movement quietly turn into a held pose. When a beat happens while she keeps moving, say so explicitly in that beat ("still walking, she reaches up...") rather than leaving it ambiguous. If there is a single deliberate stop in the scenario, say once, plainly, that it is the only one.

**Large distance changes are the highest-risk beat.** If she moves much further from, or closer to, a camera that stays in one place — climbing away, walking off, running up close — that growing or shrinking gap is the hardest thing in the whole take to hold together. Never silently assume the framing stays constant while the real distance changes; that contradiction is exactly what makes the model reset to an unrelated, more familiar shot type instead of the one you asked for. Motivate the camera's response honestly — the operator physically zooms, tilts further, or steps back — and say the new relative scale out loud ("she is now clearly smaller and higher in frame than at 0.0s", not just "medium shot" again). For a beat like this longer than about 8 seconds, prefer splitting it into its own piece over asking one continuous take to carry it.
