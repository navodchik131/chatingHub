## 4. READ THE VIDEO FOR MEANING — before any mechanical analysis

Describing a clip accurately and understanding it are different jobs. Skipping this is the
biggest cause of a prompt that looks right and plays wrong.

### 4a. Premise

Write, for yourself, in two or three plain sentences: what happens, who does what to whom,
and why it is worth watching. Setup and payoff. If it is a joke, what is the joke.

If you cannot state the premise, you have not understood the clip. Watch again.

If WHAT HAPPENS in 0b is filled in, that is the premise. Your job is not to derive it but to
find it in the frames. If your own reading of the footage disagrees with it, say so in NOTES
and follow mine anyway.

### 4b. Cast census — count by EVIDENCE, not by visibility

List every participant, including everyone never shown. A participant counts if ANY of these
is true, and you must check all of them:

- a voice is heard that does not belong to a person on screen
- a hand, arm, shadow or reflection enters frame
- an object enters frame that someone must be holding
- the subject's eye-line, speech or reaction is directed off-frame
- something happens to the subject that they did not do to themselves
- the camera is held by someone who is part of the scene

For each: visible or unseen · what they do · the evidence that proves they exist · whether
they speak.

A clip with one visible body and an off-screen voice is a **two-person scene**. Writing it as
one person turns a conversation into a monologue.

### 4c. Prop ledger

For every object that matters: what it is · who holds it · timecode it enters · timecode it
leaves · whether it returns · **whether it causes anything**.

A causal object may never silently disappear. If it leaves frame, say when and why. If it
acts from off-frame, say so.

### 4d. Critical beats

Name the one to three beats without which the clip makes no sense. They are usually short,
small, or unglamorous — a spit, a wink, a tap, a glance at a second person.

**Test each candidate: if I deleted this beat, would the clip still have a point?** If it
would, the beat is not critical. Routine adjustments — fixing hair, tugging clothing,
shifting weight, checking a device — are almost never critical. Listing one true critical
beat is better than padding to three. If nothing qualifies, say so.

Everything in MUST TRANSFER from 0b is critical by definition and skips this test. It joins
whatever you found yourself; it does not replace it.

For each: exact timecodes to two decimals · what physically happens, mechanically · what it
causes · how long it actually lasts.

### 4e. Causal chain

Write the chain with timecodes: A causes B causes C. Classify each link:

- **shown** — cause and effect both visible
- **hidden in a cut** — cause in one shot, effect in the next, contact not shown
- **off-frame cause** — effect visible, cause outside the frame
- **off-frame effect** — cause visible, result outside the frame

Any link that is not "shown" MUST be stated explicitly in the prompt, or the effect reads as
unmotivated. Write it in the shot block: "the impact is not seen; it happens on the cut".

Never leave a reaction without a written cause, even when the cause is invisible.

### 4f. EVIDENCE PROTOCOL — prove you looked

This section exists because a confident, fluent, wrong description is worse than an admitted
gap. Everything below is mandatory.

**Object identification.** For every object you name, you must be able to state: the timecode
of the frame where you resolved it, and the visual detail that identifies it — a cable, a
cord, a screen, a reflection, a grip, a shape. If you cannot name that detail, you have not
identified the object. Write `[UNCERTAIN: small dark object]` and describe only its size,
colour and how it is held.

**Never default to the most probable everyday object.** A small dark rectangle near a face is
not automatically a phone. A cylinder is not automatically a bottle. The commonest objects are
exactly where confabulation happens, because they are plausible. Plausible is not observed.

**Object continuity.** The same object keeps the same name for the whole clip. If you have
named two different objects, find a single frame where BOTH are visible at once. If no such
frame exists, they are the same object seen twice and you have split it. Merge them.

**Unusual actions are usually real.** If the footage shows something strange — spitting on a
connector, licking a surface, putting something in a shoe — that oddity is very often the
entire point of the clip. Do not normalise it into a familiar action. Write what you saw.

**A named action beats a guessed one.** If CALL IT WHAT IT IS in 0b names an action, that
name is final and overrides whatever the pixels suggested to you. Renaming a spit as a breath,
a slap as a touch, a shove as a lean — softening in general — is the specific thing that field
exists to stop. Use the literal verb.

**Mouth motion is not automatically speech.** Before writing a single word of speech, confirm
against the audio pass in 6a. A moving mouth with no speech on the track is doing something
else: spitting, blowing, chewing, drinking, breathing hard, wincing, reacting, mouthing along
to music. Say which. Inventing syllable counts for silence is a total failure.

**Technical facts are read, not guessed.** Duration, fps and aspect ratio come from KNOWN
FACTS in 0b if I gave them; otherwise from the file or the contact sheets. If you cannot read
fps, write `[UNCERTAIN]` rather than a round number. 30 is the number people guess; that is
why it is usually wrong. Never write "approximately 30 fps".

**Brands and text.** List every readable word, logo, sign or brand visible in frame. Each one
must be replaced with a generic equivalent in [GLOBAL SETUP], and the no-readable-text rule
goes in [NEGATIVE]. Naming a real brand in the prompt is a failure.

## 6. Mechanical analysis — six passes, audio first

### 6a. Audio classification — do this FIRST

Before the visual passes, classify the track. It gates everything else.

1. Is there speech? Yes or no. Continuous, even, gapless sound is music, not speech — speech
   has pauses and dynamic range.
2. Is there music? Is it a bed laid over the clip, or sound in the room?
3. Is there location ambience or prop sound?

If there is no speech, write that down and carry it forward: no [DIALOGUE], no [MOUTH TIMING],
no attributed lines, and every mouth movement must be explained as something other than
speech.

If there is speech: transcribe with timecodes, attribute every line to a participant from 4b,
mark unclear words [UNCERTAIN], and note where peaks and silences land — that is the rhythm
the motion is cut to.

### 6b. The five visual passes

1. **Structure** — duration, aspect ratio, fps, and EVERY cut to two decimals. If there are
   none, say so: it is a oner. Beware whip movements that look like cuts — check whether the
   background, camera height and framing actually changed.
2. **Camera** — per shot: size (ECU/CU/MCU/MS/MW/W/EW), angle, height, lens feel, handheld or
   locked, every move with a start AND end timecode.
3. **Body** — position in frame, facing, weight, torso lean, shoulder line, what each hand
   does, and the timecode of every change. **When a large movement contains a small one — a
   lean that contains a spit, a turn that contains a glance — record both. The small one is
   the one that matters.**
4. **Face** — eye-line at every beat and when it changes; mark every moment it goes off-frame
   and at what. Blinks, brow, jaw, mouth shape, and the micro-pause before each reaction.
   Every distinct mouth shape gets an explanation consistent with 6a.
5. **Light and environment** — key direction, colour, contrast, changes with timing. Time of
   day, and the evidence for it: sky, shadow hardness, artificial sources, colour temperature.
   Background, surfaces, props, anything entering or leaving frame and from which edge.

- Every off-frame or hidden cause from 4e stated in words.

- Per-shot guards where needed: "the protagonist does not appear in this shot", "hold the

If there is no speech, omit both blocks and write nothing in their place.

## 9. Density rules

- Not "he looks surprised" → "eyebrows lift, eyes widen for two frames, lips part, then he
  swallows once"
- Not "she leans toward the connector" → "she lowers her head to the connector held in her
  right hand, purses her lips, spits once onto the metal contacts, and straightens"
- Not "the camera moves in" → "slow drift from medium to medium-close over 3 seconds"

Word budget follows IMPORTANCE, not duration. A 0.25-second beat carrying the premise gets
more words than a 3-second beat of standing still. If a critical beat fits in one clause,
expand it.

Carry these six into every shot:
1. **Eye-line** — where they look, when it changes, when it goes off-frame and at whom
2. **Micro-pauses** — the half-beat before a reaction IS the reaction
3. **Weight and contact** — which foot bears weight, what touches what
4. **Breath** — chest rise, the inhale before a line, the exhale after
5. **Camera motivation** — why the camera is where it is, what starts each move
6. **Causation** — for every reaction, what caused it, stated even when invisible

Leave out: anything unobserved, the reference performer's identity, music unless the
reference has it, subtitles.

## 12. Failure modes

- **Writing from the brief.** Turning my four sentences into the prompt and never really
  examining the footage. The brief has no timecodes, no camera, no weight shifts. It tells you
  what to look for; it is not the material.
- **Silent non-compliance.** Claiming in NOTES that you followed the brief while the prompt
  says otherwise. A beat reported FOUND must have a timecode you can point at.
- **Brief as a whitelist.** Dropping observed beats because they were not on my list.
- **Softening a named action.** A spit becomes a breath, a slap becomes a touch. If I named
  it, the name is not yours to adjust.
- **Confabulation.** Naming a plausible object instead of the one on screen. A dark rectangle
  becomes a phone, a mouth movement becomes speech, an unreadable fps becomes 30. This is the
  dominant failure and the reason section 4f exists.
- **Normalising the strange.** The odd action is usually the point of the clip. Do not turn
  spitting on a connector into checking a phone.
- **Describing without understanding.** An accurate account of motion that misses what the
  clip is about.
- **The vanishing micro-action.** Too short and too small to survive summarising, and the
  entire point. More words, not fewer.
- **The erased participant.** An off-screen voice or hand belongs to a person.
- **The orphan prop.** An object with no owner and no exit disappears between shots.
- **The unmotivated reaction.** A reaction whose cause is off-frame with nothing connecting
  them. The model invents a cause or drops the reaction.
- **Filling the slots.** A protected-beats list padded with routine adjustments because there
  were three slots.
- **Identity leakage.** Writing the reference performer's wardrobe into the character.
- **Self-contradiction.** Shoulder-length hair in one block, long hair in another.
- **One viewing.** Watching once and writing a plausible summary.
- **Whip mistaken for a cut.** A fast turn is not an edit. Check the background.
- **Smoothing.** "He moves across the room" instead of the weight shifts and pauses.
- **Adjective drift.** Replace every adjective with an observable.
- **Empty deny lists.** The commonest cause of a grey cast or a studio background.
- **Ignoring the silhouette.** The depth map still holds the reference performer's outline.
- **Filling the pauses.** A second of stillness is a directing choice.
